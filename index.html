SLAC · Linac Coherent Light Source

Portraits of the
LCLS Instruments

LCLS is an X-ray laser that makes flashes of light a few femtoseconds long. Its instruments, each in its own experimental hutch, use those flashes to watch atoms and electrons move. These nine paintings don't show the hardware. They show what each instrument lets scientists see, and where that work shows up in everyday life.

How to read a portrait

  • Top: the science, with matter magnified. Bottom: the everyday world.
  • A violet-to-cyan streak is an X-ray pulse from LCLS.
  • A red streak is an optical laser that starts the action.
  • The ribbon joins the two halves: “this discovery leads to this.”

Each portrait is a sketch written in code with p5.brush and p5.js, with no photos or clip art. Click a painting to open the full-size image, or open its live sketch to watch it paint in your browser.

The science text is based on the instrument pages at lcls.slac.stanford.edu. Some of the everyday-life examples (such as vaccines, skin, homes, trains and medicines) are our own plain-language extensions of what those pages say.

This is a personal art project, not an official SLAC or LCLS website.
